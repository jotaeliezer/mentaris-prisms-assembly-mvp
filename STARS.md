# Stars

Bright stars sit in a layer. The ship can collect star dust and light rays from them. Those are fabrication materials. They are not ores, and they are not alien parts.

This is a plan. Layer stars are already in the belt. Flying into the core is a light bump. The first pass through the halo shakes one mote of that star's dust loose. The drifting cloud, the light rays, and the bench recipe that spends Pale dust are not built yet.

Ores come from asteroids and, later, from digging in `PLANETS.md`. Alloys are made at the Fabrication bench. Star dust and light rays are a third pile: things the ship only gets by flying to a star.

## What a star is

A star is a bright body, much smaller than the small planet in `PLANETS.md`. It is larger than a ship and smaller than a large asteroid. It stays fixed on its layer. It does not drift, and it does not travel between layers.

The ship cannot dig a star and cannot build on one. Flying into the bright core bumps the hull, a light hit, and pushes the ship back out. Collection happens in the space around the star, not inside it.

Stars are uncommon. A layer might have none. A cluster should have enough that a pilot who goes looking can find one. They must not sit on the ship at the start of a session.

## Star dust

Dust is the loose material around a star. It drifts in a wide, slow cloud, smaller than a nebula. The ship collects a mote by flying over it, the same way it collects a mineral that fell off an asteroid. Dust is not pulled into the hotbar from across the layer.

Three kinds. The star's color is the kind.

| Dust | Star | How common | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pale dust | White-blue, the ordinary bright star | The usual find | The common star material. Later recipes that should feel special, but not rare, ask for it. |
| Cinder dust | Warm amber | Less often | A step up. Costs more in a recipe than Pale dust. |
| Glint dust | Hard gold-white | Rare | The diamond of this pile. A recipe asks for a few motes, never a stack of them. |

Names match the ore ladder on purpose, so the colors already mean common, uncommon, and rare. Dust is still not the ore. Pale dust does not count as Pale ore, and it cannot pay an ore cost.

One visit shakes loose a small number of motes, then that star is quiet for a while. Camping on one star does not empty it forever, and it does not become a better mine than the belt.

## Light rays

A ray is a beam the star throws across the belt. It is long, thin, and bright. It is not a cloud of motes.

The ship collects a ray by flying across the beam. Crossing it once picks up one ray of that kind. The beam can be crossed again later, after the star has rested. Sitting inside the beam does not keep paying out.

| Ray | How it looks | How common | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soft ray | A wide, pale beam | The usual ray | The common light. Used where a recipe needs light but not much of it. |
| Hard ray | A thin, sharp beam | Less often | A focused light. Costs more than a soft ray. |
| Prism ray | A beam that splits into a short fan of color | Rare | One prism ray stands in for a large pile of common materials in the most expensive recipes. |

A star throws either dust or a ray, not a pile of both at once. Pale and cinder stars shed dust. The rare gold-white star is the one that throws a prism ray. A hard ray can come from a cinder star. A soft ray can come from an ordinary star. The exact pairing can change. The rarity order should not: pale, then cinder, then glint, and soft, then hard, then prism.

## Using them

Dust and rays are items. They go to the hotbar or the cargo. They can be stored in an ore vault once that room exists. A hull breach drops them with everything else that was on the ship.

They are ingredients, not powers. Carrying a prism ray does not light the belt, does not raise hull points, and does not skip a question.

Where they get spent:

- New fabrication recipes that should feel above the ore alloys. A later weapon, hull level, or instrument can ask for dust or a ray on top of its ore cost.
- Planet base rooms in `PLANETS.md`, added on top of the already expensive ore recipe.
- The costly station rooms in `CONSTRUCT.md` can gain a dust or ray line later. Do not remove the ore, the alloy, or the alien part to make room for it.

Hand recipes stay ore only. Prism glass and the Fabrication bench do not ask for dust or rays. If a recipe needs a ray, it needs the bench installed.

A sensible expensive line, when those recipes are chosen, looks like a large ore count, one alloy, and then a little dust or a single ray. A prism ray belongs only on the longest recipes: a vast-planet room, a layer extension, or something at that weight. Do not put a prism ray on a wall.

## What this is not

- Not a background starfield. The tiny stars already drawn behind the belt are scenery. These stars are objects the ship can fly to.
- Not a sun that burns the ship for staying in a region. The only touch is the core bump.
- Not a second ore. The colors rhyme with Pale, Amber, and Glint. The items do not stack with those ores.
- Not built yet. No star body, no mote, and no beam.
